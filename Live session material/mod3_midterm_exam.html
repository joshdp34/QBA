Overview

This exam covers the full analytics decision cycle as you’ve applied it so far this semester: framing a decision, summarizing evidence (Module 1), quantifying uncertainty with a single estimate (Module 2), and comparing groups while evaluating whether a comparison supports a causal claim (Module 3). Roughly half of the exam is weighted toward Module 3, one-sample and two-sample tests, a paired comparison, ANOVA with Tukey’s HSD, and a causality assessment, since that’s the most recent and most comprehensive material.

Everyone will work from the same dataset, so your results should be directly comparable to a correctly worked answer key, choose your own workplace data for this one.

The Dataset

You’ll use the Sample Superstore dataset, a widely used, freely available retail sales dataset (originally distributed by Tableau as sample data, now hosted in many places on Kaggle). It contains 9,994 rows and 21 columns of U.S. retail order data.

NoteWhere to get it

Search Kaggle for “Superstore Dataset” (for example, the dataset published by user vivek468, titled “Superstore Dataset Final”, is a stable, popular version) and download the CSV. You will need a free Kaggle account. Place the file in your project’s data folder, exactly as you did with readiness_store_sales.csv in Module 1.

If your downloaded version’s exact row count or column names differ slightly from what’s described below (different uploads occasionally vary a little), use whatever columns are present that match the descriptions; the core columns (Region, Segment, Category, Sub-Category, Sales, Profit, Discount, Order Date) are present in essentially every version of this dataset.

Relevant columns for this exam:

Column Description
Order ID Identifier for the order (one order can include multiple line items)
Order Date Date the order was placed
Segment Customer segment: Consumer, Corporate, or Home Office
Region Central, East, South, or West
Category Furniture, Office Supplies, or Technology
Sub-Category A more specific product grouping within Category
Sales Revenue for that line item, in dollars
Quantity Number of units in that line item
Discount Discount rate applied to that line item (0 to 1)
Profit Profit or loss for that line item, in dollars

What Your Report Must Include

Your rendered Quarto report should contain the following sections, in this order.

1. Framing (short)

In 2–3 sentences, state a business decision this analysis could inform (for example, whether the company should adjust its regional strategy, its discounting practices, or its customer-segment focus).

2. Module 1: Exploring the Data

  • State the observational unit (what does one row represent?).
  • Classify at least four variables as categorical or quantitative, and for quantitative variables, discrete or continuous.
  • Include one table (for example, a frequency table of Region or Category).
  • Include one graph, appropriately chosen for the variable type(s) involved.
  • Report at least two descriptive statistics, correctly matched to variable type.

3. Module 2: Quantifying Uncertainty for a Single Estimate

  • Probability. Compute the sample proportion of line items that are profitable (Profit > 0). Interpret it in plain language.
  • Confidence interval. Build and correctly interpret a 95% confidence interval for the true mean Profit per line item.

4. Module 3, Part A: One-Sample Hypothesis Test

Company policy assumes an average discount rate of 15% across all orders. Using your sample, test whether the true mean Discount differs from 0.15. State \(H_0\) and \(H_a\), report your test statistic and p-value, and state your conclusion at \(\alpha=0.05\).

5. Module 3, Part B: Two-Sample Independent Comparison

Compare mean Profit per line item between the Consumer and Corporate segments, treating them as two independent groups. State your hypotheses, choose and justify pooled vs. Welch’s test, report your conclusion, and construct a confidence interval for the difference in means.

6. Module 3, Part C: Paired Comparison

Identify the two most recent full calendar years present in your data (based on Order Date). For each Sub-Category, compute the mean Profit in the earlier year and the mean Profit in the later year, one pair of values per sub-category. Treat each Sub-Category as one paired observational unit, and run a paired t-test on the year-over-year difference in mean profit. Report your conclusion and a confidence interval for the mean difference.

NoteWhy this counts as a paired design

Each Sub-Category is measured twice, once in each year, the same way the same employee’s performance was measured before and after training in ?@sec-week06-03. Analyzing this as two independent samples (all sub-categories’ year-1 profits vs. all sub-categories’ year-2 profits, unpaired) would throw away the fact that the same sub-categories appear in both years, exactly the mistake that section warned about.

7. Module 3, Part D: ANOVA and Tukey’s HSD

Compare mean Profit per line item across the four Regions. Run a one-way ANOVA, report the F-statistic, degrees of freedom, and p-value, and if the result is significant, run Tukey’s HSD to identify which specific pairs of regions differ.

8. Module 3, Part E: Evaluating Causality

Using your ANOVA result from Part D and the causation checklist from ?@sec-week07-02, evaluate whether this data supports the claim that Region causes different profit levels. Explicitly address: is this an observational or experimental comparison? Identify at least one plausible confounder specific to this dataset (for example, differences in product mix, shipping costs, or discounting practices across regions). State whether you think the evidence supports a causal interpretation, and why.

9. Executive Recommendation

A short paragraph (4–6 sentences), written for a manager, not a statistician, that ties your findings together into a recommendation addressing the decision you framed in Section 1. Be explicit about the uncertainty in your findings and avoid statistical jargon.

What Is Not Required for This Exam

  • Regression, forecasting, or any predictive model (Modules 4–5)
  • Two-way ANOVA, repeated-measures ANOVA, or any comparison design beyond what’s specified above
  • A survey of every possible variable in the dataset; use only what each section asks for

Format and Length
